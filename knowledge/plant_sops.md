# Plant knowledge base (illustrative SOPs for the simulated Dahej NCB plant)

## SOP-N01 Acid dosing limit
Mixed-acid dosing into chlorobenzene is exothermic and heat-removal limited. The dosing rate must never exceed the jacket's heat duty (U x A x dT). Operators must not shorten dosing by raising the acid feed rate when the jacket cannot remove the heat: accumulation of unreacted acid is the classic route to a nitration runaway. A dosing time that is longer than the design value is a symptom to investigate, not a setpoint to override.

## SOP-M07 Jacket fouling and cleaning
Cooling-water side scaling and process-side polymer film both reduce the jacket overall heat-transfer coefficient U. Trigger: back-calculated U more than 15% below the clean design value of 450 W/m2K for two consecutive weeks. Action: schedule a chemical descaling (CIP) of the jacket and cooling-water circuit during the next planned stop, typically 16 to 24 hours of reactor downtime. Check cooling-tower water treatment (hardness, cycles of concentration) for the root cause.

## SOP-U03 Summer cooling-water operation
In Gujarat the cooling-tower supply temperature follows the wet-bulb temperature and rises from about 24 C in January to 32 C in May and June, which cuts the jacket driving force by roughly a third. Options: switch dosing to the chilled-water (brine) header in April to June, schedule the heaviest dosing in night hours when the tower runs cooler, and add tower cells or fill before summer. Each option needs a Management of Change review.

## SOP-Q02 Incoming chlorobenzene specification
Chlorobenzene moisture must be below 200 ppm (Karl Fischer). Wet feed dilutes the mixed acid, slows the nitration and raises dinitro and phenolic impurities, which shows up as QC failures and rework washes. Every incoming lot needs a certificate of analysis and an in-house KF check; repeated failures trigger a supplier corrective action request (SCAR) and quarantine of that supplier's lots.

## SOP-Q05 QC laboratory coverage
Release testing (GC purity and isomer ratio) takes about 1.5 hours per sample. On the night shift one analyst covers both the NCB and the utilities laboratory, so NCB samples queue until the analyst is free. Options: a second night analyst, or an at-line GC with automated sampling for the release test.

## SOP-L01 Export dispatch
Released batches are packed into drums or ISO tanks and trucked to Mundra port, about 20 hours door to door. Export orders carry a vessel cut-off date; missing it is a late delivery, and customer contracts apply a liquidated-damages penalty of about 2% of order value.
